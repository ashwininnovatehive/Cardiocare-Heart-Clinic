Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR)

Home  /  TAVR

Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR), also known as TAVI, is a revolutionary, minimally invasive procedure to replace a diseased aortic valve. It is designed for patients suffering from severe aortic stenosis (narrowing of the aortic valve), who may be at intermediate or high risk for traditional open-heart surgery.

This procedure allows a new valve to be inserted within the diseased native valve without the need to surgically remove the old one. TAVR can relieve the signs and symptoms of aortic stenosis and improve survival in patients who might otherwise have limited treatment options.

The TAVR Procedure

A catheter carrying a collapsible replacement valve is inserted through a small incision, typically in the groin, and guided up to the heart. Once in position, the new valve is expanded, pushing the old valve leaflets aside. The new valve begins to function immediately, regulating blood flow out of the heart.

Benefits of TAVR

  • Less invasive than open-heart surgery.
  • Faster recovery time and shorter hospital stays.
  • Significant improvement in symptoms like shortness of breath, chest pain, and fatigue.
  • A viable and life-saving option for high-risk patients.
